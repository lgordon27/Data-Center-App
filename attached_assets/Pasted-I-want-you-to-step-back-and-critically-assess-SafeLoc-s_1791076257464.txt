## What we are trying to build

SafeLoc is an evidence-backed intelligence and diligence platform for physical AI infrastructure projects.

Its core promise is straightforward:

**A user identifies a real data-center project, and SafeLoc finds relevant public information, explains what the evidence supports, shows what remains uncertain, and helps the user understand the implications.**

The arbitrary-project research workflow is the core product. It must work beyond curated demonstrations or canonical dossiers.

A user should be able to enter a project name, operator if known, and location, then receive useful, sourced intelligence without needing to know how our research pipeline works.

### 1. Useful project intelligence

The platform should investigate topics such as:

- Project identity, ownership, operator, campus, facilities, and phases.
- Announced versus operational capacity and development status.
- Grid connection, electricity supply, and power constraints.
- Water and cooling requirements.
- Permits, community concerns, and development conditions.
- Construction progress, capital commitments, and financing.
- Tenants, counterparties, and commercial dependencies.
- Physical hazards and operational vulnerabilities.

These are research topics, not a requirement to force every fact into a financial-model field.

A well-supported capacity announcement, financing event, permit condition, or construction update can be useful intelligence even when it cannot change the financial model.

### 2. Evidence that users can inspect and trust

A useful finding should let a user understand:

- What is being asserted.
- Which source and passage support it.
- Which project, facility, phase, and time period it concerns.
- Whether it is an attributed report, an established fact under our validation rules, an estimate, or unresolved.
- Whether another source contradicts it.
- When it was published and researched, where available.

We should preserve uncertainty without making uncertainty synonymous with uselessness.

An operator announcement can support “the operator announced X” without proving that X has already happened. A credible report can be shown as attributed reporting without being promoted to verified operational reality.

We must prevent unrelated campuses, phases, operators, or seeded dossier facts from contaminating the result.

### 3. Honest partial results

Public research will sometimes be incomplete. The product should still deliver what it legitimately found.

It should distinguish:

- Relevant reporting found, but not independently established.
- A category assessed with insufficient support for a conclusion.
- A category not assessed because no suitable text reached analysis.
- A category blocked by access, provider, time, or budget limitations.

“Not assessed” must not become “the public record does not disclose this,” and zero financial candidates must not imply zero useful intelligence.

### 4. Financial interpretation as a downstream capability

Evidence-backed research and financial-model eligibility are separate stages.

Some findings may eventually support model inputs. Those require appropriate units, scope, dates, evidentiary support, and existing eligibility checks. Only explicit human acceptance should change the model.

Other findings should remain useful research without being forced into one of the existing modeled inputs.

Illustrative scenarios must remain clearly labeled and optional. The default experience should not show unavailable or confusing modeling controls.

### 5. A simple user experience

The default result should answer three questions:

1. What did we find?
2. What remains uncertain or incomplete?
3. What should I do next?

Users should be able to inspect sources and reasoning, but should not need to read internal identifiers, routing diagnostics, token budgets, or lengthy repeated warnings to understand a project.

We want depth on demand, not complexity on the default screen.

### 6. Longer-term intelligence across projects

Once project-level research is reliable, the platform could compare projects across the dataset to identify patterns in capacity, development progress, financing, utility constraints, counterparties, and execution risks.

That requires consistent definitions, provenance, freshness, deduplication, and careful handling of missing information. Announced capacity cannot be silently combined with operational capacity, and campus totals cannot be double-counted with constituent phases.

This is a future direction, not authorization to build a new analytics layer now. First we need reliable project-level conversion from sources into useful findings.

An agent is optional. We do not need an agent label; we need a working research loop. If bounded adaptive research would solve a demonstrated limitation, explain precisely why.
